## Two prices: base and premium

The AMM uses two distinct prices, both derived from the protocol's NAV:

| Price | Formula | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Base price** | `NAV / live supply` | Exits (redeeming EVE for ETH) and batch pricing |
| **Premium price** | `NAV / (live supply × connector weight)` | Entries (depositing ETH to mint EVE) |

Live supply is total EVE supply minus the escrowed EVE in priced, unprocessed requests still
inside the processing window. NAV already deducts the matching ETH liability. Unpriced queued
requests remain equity. See [EVE & NAV](/how-everstrat-works/eve-and-nav).

The **connector weight** (**0.9** in the [dated deployment snapshot](/operations/current-deployments), range `(0, 1]`) is the parameter that creates the bonding
curve. At the default of 0.9, the premium price is roughly **11% above** the base price (1/0.9 ≈ 1.111),
meaning new depositors pay somewhat more per EVE than existing holders would receive on exit. The gap
accrues to existing holders, not to a fee recipient — it increases the NAV per EVE for everyone already in.

The connector weight can be adjusted by `ADMIN_ROLE` within the allowed range. At 1, the two
prices are equal. Deposits use NAV before the incoming ETH; integer rounding applies to output.

## ETH accounting and oracle dependence

Entries and exits are priced in ETH. The AMM obtains NAV from StrategyManager, which can
consult Oracle indirectly when valuing assets. Oracle data is used for:

- The bootstrap minimum deposit check (validated in USD: minimum $1,000)
- USD-denominated view functions (so the UI can display a dollar price)
- Strategy asset valuation inside `navInETH()` (for non-WETH tokens held by strategies)

Stale or invalid required feeds can make NAV unavailable and block minting, redemption,
and batch pricing. ETH denomination does not remove the oracle dependency of asset valuation.

## Transaction protections

- Minting enforces the caller's `minTokensToMint`.
- Exit submission enforces `maxTokensToBurn` for the requested ETH on both paths,
  capping immediate burning or queued escrow.
- Queued redemption compares batch price with request-time price using `priceTolerance`.
- Pause controls and fail-closed NAV can stop entry, exit submission, and batch pricing.

The deployed AMM has no rolling base-price checkpoint or 5% deviation limit. UniCL strategy
calmness and Converter swap quote checks are separate protections.

Source: [AMM at the reviewed contracts commit](https://github.com/everstrat-xyz/contracts/blob/157129b7c7f3db06275ecc6697ff0acfab3d9dbe/src/contracts/AMM.sol).

## Bootstrap rules

The first deposit into a fresh deployment must be worth at least **$1,000**
(`MIN_INITIAL_DEPOSIT_USD`). The protocol permanently mints **1 EVE** to the dead address
`0x000000000000000000000000000000000000dEaD`; the first depositor receives the remainder.
This initializes supply once per deployment. It is not a minimum applied to every later deposit.

<Accordions type="single">
  <Accordion title="Formula detail & worked example">

**Base price** (exits):

```text
base_price = NAV_total / live_supply
```

**Premium price** (entries):

```text
adjusted_supply = live_supply × connector_weight
premium_price   = NAV_total / adjusted_supply
```

At the default connector weight of 0.9:

```text
premium_price = NAV_total / (live_supply × 0.9)
              ≈ 1.111 × (NAV_total / live_supply)
              ≈ 1.111 × base_price
```

**Example.** Suppose the protocol holds 10 ETH in NAV and 1,000 EVE are in circulation (ignoring the
1 EVE minted to the dead address at bootstrap, which remains part of total supply). Assume no
priced queued offsets:

- Base price (exit) = 10 ETH / 1,000 EVE = **0.01 ETH per EVE**
- Premium price (entry, cw=0.9) = 10 ETH / (1,000 × 0.9) = 10 / 900 ≈ **0.01111 ETH per EVE**

If you deposit 1 ETH, you receive roughly 1 ETH / 0.01111 ETH/EVE ≈ **90 EVE**. The new NAV is 11 ETH,
the new supply is 1,090 EVE. The new base price is 11/1,090 ≈ **0.01009 ETH per EVE** — slightly higher
than before your deposit, so original holders benefited.

For a step-by-step walkthrough from the user's perspective, see
[Depositing & minting](/using-everstrat/depositing-and-minting).
